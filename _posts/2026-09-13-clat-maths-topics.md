[Quantitative Techniques](/blog/what-are-quantitative-techniques-in-clat/) is roughly 10–14 questions, and the mathematics does not go beyond **Class 10**. This is the complete list rather than a selection from a longer one.

## Arithmetic

- **Percentages** — increase, decrease, successive change. The single most tested topic.
- **Ratio and proportion**, including dividing a quantity in a given ratio
- **Averages**, including weighted averages
- **Profit, loss and discount**
- **Simple and compound interest** — simple far more often
- **Time, speed and distance**
- **Time and work**
- **Mixtures and alligation** — occasional

## Basic algebra

- Linear equations in one and two variables
- Simple word problems reduced to equations

Nothing beyond what a Class 10 syllabus covers.

## Data interpretation

This is the section's real subject, and everything above is a tool for it.

- **Tables** — most common
- **Bar charts**, including grouped and stacked
- **Pie charts**, including converting between percentage and degrees
- **Line graphs**
- **Caselets** — data given as prose rather than a chart

## Basic numeracy

- Fractions and decimals
- Squares, square roots and simple powers
- Elementary mensuration — area and perimeter of simple figures

## What is not tested

Trigonometry. Calculus. Coordinate geometry. Probability beyond the elementary. Permutations and combinations. Logarithms. Complex numbers. Statistics beyond mean, median and mode.

If a preparation book covers these under a CLAT heading, it is padding.

## The topic nobody lists

**Reading the data correctly.** The arithmetic in this section is not hard; identifying which figure the question refers to, under time pressure, is where marks are lost — more often than through calculation error.

Practise extraction as deliberately as you practise percentages. Read the question, find the exact row and column, then calculate. Reversing that order is the most common failure.

## A realistic target

For a candidate comfortable with arithmetic, this section is among the most reliably scorable on the paper — an answer is right or it is not, with none of the judgement [Legal Reasoning](/blog/what-is-legal-reasoning-in-clat/) demands. Aim to secure the straightforward questions and leave the hardest one or two.

For a candidate who struggles, the [honest calculation about where scarce hours belong](/blog/is-maths-necessary-for-clat/) is worth reading.
