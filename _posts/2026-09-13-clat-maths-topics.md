[Quantitative Techniques](/blog/what-are-quantitative-techniques-in-clat/) is roughly 10–14 questions, and the mathematics does not go beyond **Class 10**. The official syllabus names ratios and proportions, basic algebra, mensuration and statistical estimation. The checklist below expands these into practice skills; it is not an exhaustive official chapter list.

## Arithmetic

- **Percentages** — increase, decrease, successive change.
- **Ratio and proportion**, including dividing a quantity in a given ratio
- **Averages**, including weighted averages
- **Profit, loss and discount**
- **Simple and compound interest**
- **Time, speed and distance**
- **Time and work**
- **Mixtures and alligation**

## Basic algebra

- Linear equations in one and two variables
- Simple word problems reduced to equations

Nothing beyond what a Class 10 syllabus covers.

## Data interpretation

This is the section's real subject, and everything above is a tool for it.

- **Tables**
- **Bar charts**, including grouped and stacked
- **Pie charts**, including converting between percentage and degrees
- **Line graphs**
- **Caselets** — data given as prose rather than a chart

## Basic numeracy

- Fractions and decimals
- Squares, square roots and simple powers
- Mensuration — revise area, perimeter, surface area and volume of familiar shapes
- Statistical estimation — interpreting summaries, averages and estimates

## What is not tested

The syllabus specifies a Class 10 level. Focus on the named areas and applying them to numerical information. Do not treat an unofficial list as proof that a particular school topic can never appear.

## The topic nobody lists

**Read the data correctly.** Identify the required quantity, units, base value and relevant figures before calculating. Both extraction and calculation errors cost marks; use your own mistakes to decide which needs more practice.

## A realistic target

Aim for accurate solutions, then work on time. Compare the return from practice across all sections rather than automatically abandoning the smallest one.
