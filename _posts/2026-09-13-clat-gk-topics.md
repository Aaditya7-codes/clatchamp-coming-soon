[Current Affairs including General Knowledge](/blog/what-is-current-affairs-and-gk-in-clat/) is roughly 28–32 questions — about a quarter of CLAT. Here is what the passages actually cover.

## The categories

### National affairs
Government decisions and policy, major legislation, significant appointments, Supreme Court and High Court judgments of public importance, centre–state matters, elections and their outcomes.

The legal and constitutional end of this is disproportionately represented. It is a law entrance exam, and the passages reflect that.

### International affairs
India's bilateral relationships and treaties, multilateral groupings — UN bodies, G20, BRICS, SCO, QUAD — conflicts and peace processes, and global summits with outcomes worth reporting.

### Economy and business
Budget and its main provisions, monetary policy and rate decisions, inflation and growth figures, major mergers and regulatory action, trade agreements.

You need the **vocabulary** more than the numbers: what a fiscal deficit is, what the repo rate does. Passages assume the terms and explain the story.

### Legal and constitutional developments
New laws and amendments, landmark judgments, judicial appointments, regulatory changes. This is the category most likely to appear, and the one where reading properly pays most.

### Science, technology and environment
Space missions, health and public-health developments, climate agreements and conferences, significant technology policy.

### Awards, honours and sport
The thinnest category. Major international awards, national honours, and headline sporting outcomes. Worth a light pass, not a deep one.

## How far back?

Broadly the **twelve months before the paper**, weighted heavily towards the most recent six to eight.

For CLAT 2027 — the paper on 6 December 2026 — that means news from roughly December 2025 onward matters, with the greatest emphasis on April to November 2026.

This is the one section where starting too early has diminishing returns. Current affairs from 2024 is simply no longer current.

## Where static knowledge fits

Underneath the current story, not beside it. Passages assume background: which body administers a treaty, which article a case concerns. That supporting layer is worth learning; the capitals-and-currencies layer is not. The split is set out in [is static GK important for CLAT?](/blog/is-static-gk-important-for-clat/)

## The format point that matters most

Every one of these categories reaches you as a **450-word passage**, not as a bullet. A candidate who has memorised the categories above as fact lists has prepared for a different question type than the one the paper asks.

Practise in passage form. [How to prepare current affairs for CLAT](/blog/how-to-prepare-current-affairs-for-clat/) explains how.
