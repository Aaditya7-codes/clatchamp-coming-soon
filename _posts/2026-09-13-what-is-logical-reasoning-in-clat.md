Logical Reasoning in CLAT is **passage-based argument analysis**. You read a short piece of reasoning — about 450 words in the published syllabus — and answer questions about how it works: what it concludes, what it assumes, what would weaken it.

It accounts for roughly 22–26 of the 120 questions, about a fifth of the paper.

## The misconception worth correcting immediately

Most aspirants arrive expecting puzzles: seating arrangements, blood relations, coding-decoding, syllogisms in grid form. That is the logical reasoning of banking and SSC exams, and a great deal of the material sold for those exams gets recommended for CLAT.

**Prioritise the published skills.** The syllabus emphasises arguments, inferences, relationships, analogies, contradictions and equivalence. Generic puzzle drills should not displace practice aligned with those skills.

## The question types that actually appear

**Main conclusion.** What is the author ultimately arguing? Distinguish the conclusion from the evidence offered for it.

**Assumption.** What must be true, but is left unsaid, for the argument to hold? The test: negate the option. For a necessary-assumption question, an option whose negation undermines the argument may identify the required premise. Sufficient-assumption questions require a different test.

**Strengthen and weaken.** Which new fact makes the conclusion more or less likely? Note that weakening an argument is not the same as contradicting its conclusion.

**Inference.** What follows from the passage? Match the answer to the strength of the evidence, rather than choosing whichever sounds most cautious.

**Flaw or method.** What is wrong with the reasoning, or how is it structured? For example, an argument may treat correlation as proof of causation.

## Why it rewards the same skill as English

Both sections are reading tests with different questions attached. A candidate who reads carefully and holds an argument's structure in their head does well in both. That is also why [reading practice](/blog/how-to-prepare-for-clat-english/) pays across more of the paper than any other single activity.

The overlap with [Legal Reasoning](/blog/what-is-legal-reasoning-in-clat/) is real too: applying a principle to facts and testing whether a conclusion follows from premises are close cousins.

## A habit that helps

After each passage, before looking at the options, state the argument in one sentence: *"The author concludes X, because Y."* If that is difficult, locate the claim and supporting evidence before choosing an option.
