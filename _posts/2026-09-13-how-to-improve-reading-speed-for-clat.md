**Improve reading speed for CLAT by practising comprehension on fresh passages and tracking accuracy alongside time.** Faster reading is useful only when it supports answering correctly within the overall exam budget.

## Measure a starting point

Choose an unseen passage with a known word count and questions. Time the first reading, then separately record answering and any rereading time. Mark the answers and note where understanding failed.

Words per minute equals word count divided by reading minutes. For example, 450 words in 2.5 minutes is **180 WPM**. This is an arithmetic example, not an official target.

## Check the full set, not just reading speed

If reading takes 2.5 minutes and five questions take 45 seconds each, the set takes **6.25 minutes** before other checks. Twenty such sets would take 125 minutes and contain only 100 questions.

That is why you cannot turn one reading target into a complete paper plan. Set lengths and question counts vary; use your mock's actual sections and a budget that fits the exam.

## Practise with a purpose

Read fiction and non-fiction across familiar and unfamiliar topics. After a passage, state its central idea and how the paragraphs connect. Check that summary against the text.

For incorrect answers, identify whether the problem was vocabulary, a missed qualifier, inference or rushing. Practise the relevant skill before simply trying to go faster.

## Rereading is allowed

Returning to a particular sentence can resolve an uncertainty. Aim to reduce unfocused rereading, not to prohibit all rereading. Likewise, do not assume a pacing technique works because it raises WPM: check what it does to accuracy and total set time.

## Set a realistic routine

Try a short reading session on most days and a timed passage check each week. Use several fresh passages of comparable difficulty to assess progress; a single five-question score is noisy.

There is no fixed improvement timeline. Continue working on comprehension even if you have six months or less, while addressing other gaps revealed by practice.
