**There is no official or proven number of mocks for CLAT.** What matters is how many you review properly. One reviewed mock teaches more than three taken and forgotten.

## The short answer

For most students, one full-length mock a week is a sensible baseline, with more in the last month. This is an editorial suggestion, not a rule. If you cannot review a mock properly, take fewer.

## A cadence by stage

| Stage | Suggested rhythm |
|---|---|
| Early preparation | Section practice daily; one full mock every two or three weeks |
| Middle | One full mock a week, plus sectional tests |
| Last month | Two or three full mocks a week, plus lighter review days |
| Final week | One light mock at most; no new material |

If your exam is soon, our [last 60 days plan](/blog/clat-study-plan-last-60-days/) fits this rhythm to the calendar.

## Review is where the learning happens

After each mock, review every wrong answer and every lucky guess. Spend at least as long on the review as on the test. Label each error (misread, did not know, ran out of time, over-inferred) and look for repeats. Our [mock analysis guide](/blog/how-to-analyse-a-clat-mock-test/) shows how.

## Take them like the real exam

Take full mocks in 120 minutes, in one sitting, using +1 and −0.25 marking. Try the real 2:00 to 4:00 p.m. slot in the last weeks. A mock taken with pauses, or with answers checked mid-way, tells you little.

## How to read your scores

One score is noise. Mocks differ in difficulty, and difficulty differs from the real paper. Compare the trend over three or four mocks, and compare **accuracy and time by section**, not just the total. Do not compare your mock score with other people's ranks as if it were your CLAT rank.

## Signs you need fewer mocks and more repair

- Your errors repeat from mock to mock.
- You do not review the previous mock before taking the next.
- Your accuracy is falling because you are tired.
