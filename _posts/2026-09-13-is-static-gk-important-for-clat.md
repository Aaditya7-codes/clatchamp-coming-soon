**Yes, static GK can matter in CLAT.** The syllabus includes historical events of continuing significance as well as current events, arts and culture and international affairs. It does not publish a fixed split between static and current knowledge.

## Why background matters

A news passage can assume knowledge it does not explain. Understanding an institution, a historical development or a cultural reference can help answer a question connected to the passage.

Passage-based presentation does not mean every answer can be extracted from the text. Practise both comprehension and factual recall.

## What to include

Use the syllabus and the stories you study to identify useful background: how public institutions work, significant international organisations, cultural developments, relevant history and basic economic concepts.

Treat these as starting themes, not an exhaustive list or a guarantee of likely questions. Do not automatically discard geography, sport or a historical date when it explains a significant event.

## Avoid an invented percentage

There is no basis here for saying static GK deserves exactly one-fifth of your preparation time. Use your question practice to identify gaps, then balance new current affairs with revision of background knowledge.

Nor should background be studied once and left alone. Revisit it when later news or a wrong answer exposes a missing connection.

## A practical study method

For each substantial story, write three questions: what happened, what background explains it, and which facts could be tested? Check the answers against reliable sources and record the source date.

Return to those questions without your notes, then try fresh passage sets. This helps distinguish understanding the story from merely recognising its headline.
