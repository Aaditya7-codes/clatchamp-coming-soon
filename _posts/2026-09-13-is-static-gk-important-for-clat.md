**Static GK matters less in CLAT than in almost any other competitive exam — but it is not worthless.** The distinction that makes this useful is *which* static knowledge pays.

## What changed

CLAT used to reward memorisation: capitals, currencies, first-and-longest-highest lists, dates. Since the paper became comprehension-led, [Current Affairs including General Knowledge](/blog/what-is-current-affairs-and-gk-in-clat/) has been **passage-based**. You read roughly 450 words of news writing and answer questions on it.

That change moved the section away from recall and towards understanding. A candidate who has memorised a thousand facts but cannot read a news passage closely will underperform one who reads well and knows fewer facts.

## Where static knowledge still earns its keep

Passages assume context they do not supply. A piece on a new trade agreement may not explain which body administers the existing one. A passage on a constitutional case may not spell out which article is at issue.

So the static knowledge that pays is the kind that **sits underneath current stories**:

- The Constitution: fundamental rights, key articles, the basic structure of government
- How Parliament and the judiciary work, and the offices within them
- Major international organisations — the UN and its agencies, WTO, IMF, World Bank — and what each does
- Landmark constitutional cases, by significance rather than citation
- Basic economic vocabulary: inflation, fiscal deficit, repo rate

That is a modest list, and it is learnable in a few weeks.

## Where it does not

- Capitals, currencies, national animals
- "Firsts" — first woman to do X, first Indian to do Y
- Dates of events with no current relevance
- Long lists of dams, ports, national parks

This material fills a large share of general knowledge books, and it is mostly the wrong preparation for this paper.

## The practical split

If Current Affairs is a quarter of your paper, treat static knowledge as a **supporting layer** — perhaps a fifth of the time you give the section, front-loaded and then left alone. The other four-fifths belongs to reading current news properly and practising it [in passage form](/blog/how-to-prepare-current-affairs-for-clat/), which is the shape the exam uses.

## A test for whether a fact is worth learning

Ask: *could this plausibly appear in a news passage in the next year as assumed background?*

The name of the body that adjudicates trade disputes — yes. The longest river in Asia — no.

It is a rough filter, and it will save you months.
