**CLAT has 120 questions, carrying 120 marks, to be answered in 120 minutes.** All are multiple-choice and spread across five sections. You may leave questions unanswered.

## The split

| Section | Questions | Share |
|---|---|---|
| Current Affairs, including General Knowledge | 28–32 | ~25% |
| Legal Reasoning | 28–32 | ~25% |
| English Language | 22–26 | ~20% |
| Logical Reasoning | 22–26 | ~20% |
| Quantitative Techniques | 10–14 | ~10% |

The Consortium publishes weightage rather than a fixed count, so the exact numbers move a little from year to year. Plan against the ranges, not a single figure.

## What that leaves you per question

The average is one minute per question, **including reading and answer-sheet work**. It is not an individual deadline.

Passages share reading time across their questions, and numerical sets need calculation time. The official format does not specify a fixed number of passages. For an illustration, 24 sets of five questions would make 120 questions, but that is arithmetic, not a prediction of the paper. Use a section budget totalling 120 minutes rather than assuming every set takes six minutes.

## The distribution matters for preparation

Legal Reasoning and Current Affairs together are **half the paper**. Quantitative Techniques is a tenth. Use the section weights alongside your current accuracy and the time needed to improve; study hours need not match marks in a fixed ratio.

That does not mean abandoning Quantitative Techniques — ten to fourteen marks decides admissions at the margin. It means being clear-eyed about where an extra hour returns most.
