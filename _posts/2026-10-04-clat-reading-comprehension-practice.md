**Most CLAT sections give you a passage and ask questions on it, so reading comprehension is one skill tested in several sections, not only in English.** Improving it lifts your marks in English, Legal Reasoning, Logical Reasoning and Current Affairs, and affects the data sets in Quantitative Techniques.

## Where it shows up

- **English Language:** comprehension of prose passages.
- **Legal Reasoning:** a passage stating a rule, then a fact situation to apply it to.
- **Logical Reasoning:** argument passages, where you find the conclusion or the gap.
- **Current Affairs and GK:** passages of news and background.
- **Quantitative Techniques:** data inside a passage, table or chart.

## A reading method to test

This is a practice suggestion, not an official method. Try it on three or four passages and keep it only if your accuracy holds.

1. **Read once for the point.** Ask: what is the author trying to say, and why?
2. **Mark the turns.** Note words such as "but", "however", "therefore", which usually change the direction.
3. **Read each question's task.** "Which statement weakens" and "which statement is supported" need different answers from the same passage.
4. **Go back to the exact lines.** Confirm the option in the text, not from memory.

Test whether reading the questions first helps you. For some students it saves time; for others it breaks concentration.

## Mistakes that cost marks

- **Using outside knowledge.** The answer comes from the passage, even where you know more.
- **Over-reading.** Choosing an option that is true in general but not stated or implied.
- **Missing a qualifier.** "Some", "most", "always" and "only" change the answer.
- **Answering a different question.** Reading "which is NOT stated" as "which is stated".
- **Spending too long.** One hard passage can use up the time of two easy ones.

## A routine you can sustain

- **Daily:** one passage from a mix of sources (editorials, law and policy writing), then answer the questions or write the main point in one sentence.
- **Twice a week:** time yourself on a set, using the same per-set time you plan to use in the exam.
- **Weekly:** review every error and label the cause.

## An error log

After each set, write one line for each wrong answer and label it: **misread the question, over-inferred, used outside knowledge, ran out of time, or did not know the word.** After two weeks the labels that repeat show what to fix.
