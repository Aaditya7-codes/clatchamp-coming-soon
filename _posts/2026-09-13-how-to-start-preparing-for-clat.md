Start CLAT preparation by understanding the paper, trying every section and building a realistic routine of reading, knowledge revision and question practice.

Here is a suggested first month; compress or extend it according to the time available.

## Week one: understand the exam

Use the first few sessions to understand the format and sample the work.

- Read [the exam pattern](/blog/clat-exam-pattern/) and [the syllabus](/blog/clat-syllabus/). Learn the five sections and their weights.
- Read [one representative question from each section](/blog/what-type-of-questions-are-asked-in-clat/).
- Sit **one full past paper**, untimed, without preparing. It will be uncomfortable and that is the point — you now have a baseline and you know what the questions feel like.

Use the current syllabus to guide practice, especially for [Logical Reasoning](/blog/what-is-logical-reasoning-in-clat/), whose published focus is passage-based argument analysis.

## Weeks two to four: build two habits

Two, not ten. Habits that survive are small.

**Habit one — daily reading.** One article, then the argument in one sentence. Fifteen minutes. Six days a week. This is the foundation of [English](/blog/how-to-prepare-for-clat-english/), and it pays across the whole paper.

**Habit two — daily current affairs.** Ten minutes scanning national, international, legal and economic news. Not memorising. Building the context [the GK passages assume](/blog/what-is-current-affairs-and-gk-in-clat/).

Those two habits total 25 minutes. Add short practice sets across the five sections and time to review them; this is a foundation routine, not a complete daily workload for every candidate.

## What to deliberately ignore for now

- **Frequent full mocks without review.** After the diagnostic, use sectional work to address gaps before repeating whole papers too often.
- **Vocabulary lists.** Low return, and they crowd out reading.
- **Buying more material.** The usual first-month displacement activity.
- **Advanced material before foundations.** Sample Quantitative Techniques early so that missing maths basics can be addressed.

## Month two onwards

Once the two habits hold, add one section at a time. [Legal Reasoning](/blog/how-to-prepare-for-clat-legal-reasoning/) can be one starting point, but use your diagnostic rather than a fixed subject priority.

Start an **error log** the day you begin practising questions. For each wrong answer, one line on *why you chose wrong*. Review entries regularly to help decide where more practice would be useful. See [how to analyse a CLAT mock test](/blog/how-to-analyse-a-clat-mock-test/).

## Set the schedule to your worst week, not your best

A plan that fits only an unusually free day is difficult to maintain.

Set a daily minimum you could complete on your worst day — a busy school day. Rest when ill. Twenty minutes, perhaps. Do more when you can. But the floor is what compounds, and review whether the routine is helping you understand and answer more accurately.

## How long do you need?

Depends on your runway. [How many months are enough for CLAT preparation?](/blog/how-many-months-are-enough-for-clat-preparation/) gives honest plans for six, twelve and twenty-four months.
