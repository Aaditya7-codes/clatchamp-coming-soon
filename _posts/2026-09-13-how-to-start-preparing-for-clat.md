Most CLAT preparation fails in the first month — not from lack of effort, but from starting with the wrong things and burning out on a schedule nobody could keep.

Here is a first month that works.

## Week one: understand the exam

Do almost no studying. Spend the week finding out what you are preparing for.

- Read [the exam pattern](/blog/clat-exam-pattern/) and [the syllabus](/blog/clat-syllabus/). Learn the five sections and their weights.
- Read [one representative question from each section](/blog/what-type-of-questions-are-asked-in-clat/).
- Sit **one full past paper**, untimed, without preparing. It will be uncomfortable and that is the point — you now have a baseline and you know what the questions feel like.

Candidates who skip this spend months preparing for an exam they have imagined. The commonest casualty is [Logical Reasoning](/blog/what-is-logical-reasoning-in-clat/): months of seating-arrangement puzzles that this paper does not contain.

## Weeks two to four: build two habits

Two, not ten. Habits that survive are small.

**Habit one — daily reading.** One article, then the argument in one sentence. Fifteen minutes. Six days a week. This is the foundation of [English](/blog/how-to-prepare-for-clat-english/), and it pays across the whole paper.

**Habit two — daily current affairs.** Ten minutes scanning national, international, legal and economic news. Not memorising. Building the context [the GK passages assume](/blog/what-is-current-affairs-and-gk-in-clat/).

Twenty-five minutes a day. That is the whole prescription for month one.

## What to deliberately ignore for now

- **Mock tests.** Aside from the diagnostic in week one. You have nothing to measure yet.
- **Vocabulary lists.** Low return, and they crowd out reading.
- **Buying more material.** The usual first-month displacement activity.
- **Quantitative Techniques.** Ten per cent of the paper. It can wait a month.

## Month two onwards

Once the two habits hold, add one section at a time. [Legal Reasoning](/blog/how-to-prepare-for-clat-legal-reasoning/) first — it is the largest section and the most learnable, being technique rather than knowledge.

Start an **error log** the day you begin practising questions. For each wrong answer, one line on *why you chose wrong*. Within two months it will tell you where your time belongs, more accurately than any general advice. See [how to analyse a CLAT mock test](/blog/how-to-analyse-a-clat-mock-test/).

## Set the schedule to your worst week, not your best

The commonest error is designing a plan for an ideal Sunday. It survives a fortnight.

Set a daily minimum you could complete on your worst day — a day with a test, a family obligation, illness. Twenty minutes, perhaps. Do more when you can. But the floor is what compounds, because comprehension responds to frequency far more than to duration.

## How long do you need?

Depends on your runway. [How many months are enough for CLAT preparation?](/blog/how-many-months-are-enough-for-clat-preparation/) gives honest plans for six, twelve and twenty-four months.
