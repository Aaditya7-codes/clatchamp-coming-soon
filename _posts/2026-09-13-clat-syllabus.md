The CLAT syllabus is defined less by topics than by **skills**. The Consortium does not publish a chapter list the way a board exam does; it publishes the five areas the paper tests and the approximate weight of each.

That distinction matters. There is no syllabus to "finish" here — there is a set of abilities to build.

## The five sections

### English Language — 22–26 questions, ~20%
Comprehension from 450-word passages: main idea, inference, tone, vocabulary in context, structure. No standalone grammar.
→ [What is the English section in CLAT?](/blog/what-is-the-english-section-in-clat/) · [English topics](/blog/clat-english-topics/)

### Current Affairs including General Knowledge — 28–32 questions, ~25%
News-based passages from roughly the preceding twelve months, with some static knowledge where it supports a current story.
→ [What is Current Affairs & GK in CLAT?](/blog/what-is-current-affairs-and-gk-in-clat/) · [GK topics](/blog/clat-gk-topics/)

### Legal Reasoning — 28–32 questions, ~25%
A principle and a fact situation; apply one to the other. No prior legal knowledge required or rewarded.
→ [What is Legal Reasoning in CLAT?](/blog/what-is-legal-reasoning-in-clat/) · [Legal Reasoning topics](/blog/legal-reasoning-topics-for-clat/)

### Logical Reasoning — 22–26 questions, ~20%
Argument analysis: conclusions, assumptions, strengthen and weaken, flaws. Not puzzles or seating arrangements.
→ [What is Logical Reasoning in CLAT?](/blog/what-is-logical-reasoning-in-clat/) · [Logical Reasoning syllabus](/blog/clat-logical-reasoning-syllabus/)

### Quantitative Techniques — 10–14 questions, ~10%
Data interpretation on Class 10 arithmetic: ratio, percentage, averages, basic algebra.
→ [What are Quantitative Techniques in CLAT?](/blog/what-are-quantitative-techniques-in-clat/) · [Maths topics](/blog/clat-maths-topics/)

## The through-line

Every section is comprehension-led. You will read roughly **20 to 24 passages** in two hours, whatever the label on top. A candidate who reads well and carefully has an advantage in all five sections; a candidate who has memorised a great deal has an advantage in part of one.

This is why we keep pointing back at reading. It is not a stylistic preference — it is where the marks are.

## What is *not* on the syllabus

- Standalone grammar and error-spotting
- Seating arrangements, blood relations, coding-decoding
- Mathematics beyond Class 10
- Case law, statutes, or any legal knowledge you are expected to bring

A good deal of material marketed for CLAT covers these anyway. It is preparing you for a different exam.
