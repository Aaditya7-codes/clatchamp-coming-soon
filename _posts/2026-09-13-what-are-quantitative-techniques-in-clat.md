Quantitative Techniques is the smallest section of CLAT — roughly **10–14 of the 120 questions**, about a tenth of the paper. It gives you a short passage containing data, usually a table, chart or a few sentences of figures, and asks questions based on it.

The mathematics involved does not go beyond **Class 10 arithmetic**.

## What is actually tested

- Ratio and proportion
- Percentages, including increase and decrease
- Averages
- Basic algebra
- Reading data from tables, bar charts and pie charts
- Simple interest, profit and loss, time and work

That is close to the whole list. There is no trigonometry, no calculus, no coordinate geometry, no probability of any depth.

## Why the section frightens people more than it should

"Is maths necessary for CLAT" is one of the most searched questions about this exam, and the anxiety behind it is out of proportion to ten questions on Class 10 arithmetic. We have answered it separately and at length in [is maths necessary for CLAT?](/blog/is-maths-necessary-for-clat/) — the short version is that you do not need to have taken maths in Class 11 and 12, and plenty of students who dropped it score well here.

## The section is a reading test too

This is the part candidates miss. The arithmetic is easy; extracting the right numbers from the passage is the actual task. A question may give you five figures and require two. Misreading which column a figure sits in costs the mark just as surely as an arithmetic slip, and it is the more common error by a wide margin.

So the skill to build is not speed of calculation. It is careful extraction — the same attentiveness the rest of [the paper](/blog/clat-exam-pattern/) rewards.

## Is it worth the time?

Ten to fourteen marks is not nothing, and the questions are among the most reliably scorable on the paper — the answer is either right or it is not, with none of the judgement that Legal or Logical Reasoning demands. For a candidate comfortable with arithmetic, this section is a dependable source of marks.

For a candidate who genuinely struggles, the honest calculation is different. Ten questions at one mark each, against twenty-eight in [Legal Reasoning](/blog/what-is-legal-reasoning-in-clat/), tells you where a scarce hour of study belongs.
