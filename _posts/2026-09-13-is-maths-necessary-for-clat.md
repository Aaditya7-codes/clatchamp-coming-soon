**Yes, CLAT has a maths section. No, you do not need to have taken maths in Class 11 or 12.**

Both halves of that are true, and the gap between them is where most of the anxiety lives.

## What is actually asked

Quantitative Techniques is roughly **10–14 questions out of 120** — about a tenth of the paper. The mathematics does not go beyond **Class 10**:

- Ratio and proportion
- Percentages
- Averages
- Basic algebra
- Simple interest, profit and loss, time and work
- Reading tables, bar charts and pie charts

The official areas include ratios, basic algebra, **mensuration and statistical estimation** at Class 10 level. Treat this as a guide to preparation, not an exhaustive promise about which topics can never appear.

## Why humanities students worry more than they need to

A student who dropped maths after Class 10 often assumes they have fallen two years behind. They have not — those two years covered material CLAT does not examine. The relevant syllabus stopped where they stopped.

What *is* true is that arithmetic gets rusty without use. A student who has not calculated a percentage in two years will be slower, not less capable. Diagnose the missing skills and revise them; the time needed varies.

## The part nobody mentions

**This section needs reading and calculation.** The data arrives inside a passage, and one possible error is pulling the wrong figure from the table. Misreading which column a number sits in loses the mark exactly as a calculation slip would, so practise both.

So the skill to build is careful extraction under time pressure, which is the same skill [the rest of the paper](/blog/clat-exam-pattern/) rewards.

## If you genuinely struggle with numbers

Here is the honest calculation. Quantitative Techniques is worth 10–14 marks. [Legal Reasoning](/blog/what-is-legal-reasoning-in-clat/) is worth 28–32, and [Current Affairs](/blog/what-is-current-affairs-and-gk-in-clat/) the same. The published format does not list separate sectional qualifying cut-offs; questions contribute to the total score.

That means a candidate who is weak at arithmetic is not shut out. Other sections contribute to the total too, but the number outside Quant varies with the section split.

It does not mean abandoning the section. Ten marks decides admissions at the margin, and focused maths practice may recover useful marks. Secure the straightforward ones; do not burn fifteen minutes on the hardest.

## The short answer

If you are choosing whether to take maths in Class 11 for the sake of CLAT: **you do not need to.** Choose subjects on their own merits. You will still need to practise the maths the entrance exam covers.

## Quick answers

**Does CLAT have maths?** Yes. Quantitative Techniques is roughly 10–14 of the 120 questions.

**Is maths compulsory for CLAT?** The section is part of the paper, but you do not need maths as a Class 11 or 12 subject to be eligible. The mathematics stays at Class 10 level.

**Can I take CLAT without maths in Class 12?** Yes. Eligibility does not depend on having studied maths. You will still need to practise the section itself.

**Do I have to attempt the maths questions?** You may leave questions unanswered, and the published format does not list separate sectional cut-offs. The section above weighs what it is worth against the rest of the paper.
