**Yes, CLAT has a maths section. No, you do not need to have taken maths in Class 11 or 12.**

Both halves of that are true, and the gap between them is where most of the anxiety lives.

## What is actually asked

Quantitative Techniques is roughly **10–14 questions out of 120** — about a tenth of the paper. The mathematics does not go beyond **Class 10**:

- Ratio and proportion
- Percentages
- Averages
- Basic algebra
- Simple interest, profit and loss, time and work
- Reading tables, bar charts and pie charts

There is no trigonometry, no calculus, no coordinate geometry, no statistics of any depth. If you passed Class 10 maths, you have already been taught everything this section tests.

## Why humanities students worry more than they need to

A student who dropped maths after Class 10 often assumes they have fallen two years behind. They have not — those two years covered material CLAT does not examine. The relevant syllabus stopped where they stopped.

What *is* true is that arithmetic gets rusty without use. A student who has not calculated a percentage in two years will be slower, not less capable. That is a practice problem, and a few weeks of regular work closes it.

## The part nobody mentions

**This section is mostly a reading test.** The data arrives inside a passage, and the common error is not arithmetic — it is pulling the wrong figure from the table. Misreading which column a number sits in loses the mark exactly as a calculation slip would, and it happens far more often.

So the skill to build is careful extraction under time pressure, which is the same skill [the rest of the paper](/blog/clat-exam-pattern/) rewards.

## If you genuinely struggle with numbers

Here is the honest calculation. Quantitative Techniques is worth 10–14 marks. [Legal Reasoning](/blog/what-is-legal-reasoning-in-clat/) is worth 28–32, and [Current Affairs](/blog/what-is-current-affairs-and-gk-in-clat/) the same. There are **no sectional cut-offs** in CLAT — every question merges into one score out of 120.

That means a candidate who is weak at arithmetic is not shut out. They can be carried by the other 106 questions.

It does not mean abandoning the section. Ten marks decides admissions at the margin, and these are among the most reliably scorable questions on the paper — right or wrong, with none of the judgement Legal Reasoning demands. Secure the straightforward ones; do not burn fifteen minutes on the hardest.

## The short answer

If you are choosing whether to take maths in Class 11 for the sake of CLAT: **you do not need to.** Choose subjects on their own merits. This exam will not punish you for it.
