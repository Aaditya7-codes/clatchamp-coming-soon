The English Language section of CLAT is a **comprehension test**. You read a passage of roughly 450 words and answer questions about what it says, what it implies, and how it says it. It is about 22–26 of the 120 questions, a fifth of the paper.

It is not presented as a standalone grammar paper. The published syllabus emphasises comprehension and language skills through fiction and non-fiction passages; use that focus to guide preparation.

## What the questions ask

**Main idea.** What is the passage centrally about? Reliably harder than it sounds, because the most *memorable* part of a passage is often not its main point.

**Inference.** What follows from the passage without being stated? Choose the answer supported by the text, whether its wording is strong or qualified.

**Tone and attitude.** Is the author sceptical, approving, neutral, resigned? This is where careful readers separate from fast ones.

**Vocabulary in context.** What does this word mean *here*? Note the qualifier — the question is about usage in the passage, not the dictionary entry.

**Structure and purpose.** Why has the author included this paragraph? What function does the example serve?

## Why vocabulary lists are not the lever people think

A vocabulary list alone does not practise interpreting words in a passage. Vocabulary appears mostly as *meaning in context*, which rewards reading over memorisation ; check how a definition fits the sentence and surrounding argument.

That does not make vocabulary worthless. It makes it a by-product of reading rather than a substitute for it.

## What actually moves the score

Reading. Regularly, attentively, and on material harder than you find comfortable. Develop these skills over the time available while practising the other sections too. Improvement varies; use fresh passages to check it rather than assuming a fixed timeline.

The practical version of this is in [how to prepare for CLAT English](/blog/how-to-prepare-for-clat-english/), which combines reading, questions and answer review.

## The section that pays twice

English comprehension is not confined to the English section. [Legal Reasoning](/blog/what-is-legal-reasoning-in-clat/) gives you a principle in dense prose. [Logical Reasoning](/blog/what-is-logical-reasoning-in-clat/) gives you an argument to unpick. [Current Affairs](/blog/what-is-current-affairs-and-gk-in-clat/) uses a passage of up to 450 words. Even [Quantitative Techniques](/blog/what-are-quantitative-techniques-in-clat/) requires extracting the right figure from a paragraph.

Reading supports these tasks, while factual knowledge, mathematical operations and reasoning also need practice.
