The English Language section of CLAT is a **comprehension test**. You read a passage of roughly 450 words and answer questions about what it says, what it implies, and how it says it. It is about 22–26 of the 120 questions, a fifth of the paper.

What it is not is a grammar paper. There are no standalone error-spotting exercises, no fill-in-the-blank tense questions, no sentence correction of the kind that dominates other competitive exams.

## What the questions ask

**Main idea.** What is the passage centrally about? Reliably harder than it sounds, because the most *memorable* part of a passage is often not its main point.

**Inference.** What follows from the passage without being stated? The correct answer is nearly always the cautious one.

**Tone and attitude.** Is the author sceptical, approving, neutral, resigned? This is where careful readers separate from fast ones.

**Vocabulary in context.** What does this word mean *here*? Note the qualifier — the question is about usage in the passage, not the dictionary entry.

**Structure and purpose.** Why has the author included this paragraph? What function does the example serve?

## Why vocabulary lists are not the lever people think

Learning a thousand words is the most commonly recommended English preparation, and it is not the best use of your time. Vocabulary appears mostly as *meaning in context*, which rewards reading over memorisation — a word you have met three times in real sentences is more usable than a word you have seen once on a list.

That does not make vocabulary worthless. It makes it a by-product of reading rather than a substitute for it.

## What actually moves the score

Reading. Regularly, attentively, and on material harder than you find comfortable. The section is testing a skill that takes months to build and cannot be crammed — which is the argument for starting English early even if you start nothing else.

The practical version of this is in [how to prepare for CLAT English](/blog/how-to-prepare-for-clat-english/), which is the most-searched preparation question about this exam — three times more than the equivalent question about Legal Reasoning.

## The section that pays twice

English comprehension is not confined to the English section. [Legal Reasoning](/blog/what-is-legal-reasoning-in-clat/) gives you a principle in dense prose. [Logical Reasoning](/blog/what-is-logical-reasoning-in-clat/) gives you an argument to unpick. [Current Affairs](/blog/what-is-current-affairs-and-gk-in-clat/) gives you a 450-word news passage. Even [Quantitative Techniques](/blog/what-are-quantitative-techniques-in-clat/) requires extracting the right figure from a paragraph.

Improve your reading and you have improved at most of the paper. No other single skill does that.
